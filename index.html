Phase 2 · what's different from Phase 1

What changed

Same three lifting days, same app, same logging. Your old numbers carry over for every lift that's still in the program.

▼

More fat loss

More leg and glute work (the biggest muscles burn the most), accessories paired into supersets so sessions are denser, and two structured easy-cardio days (Tue/Thu).

↔

More mobility

Ankle rocks added to the warm-up, a paused squat for depth, a split squat that stretches the hip flexor under load, a 4-minute cooldown after every lift, and a full 15–20 min Mobility Flow on the off days.

◉

Elbow stays protected

No loaded lockouts (fly and neutral-grip presses stay), a neutral-grip pulldown instead of the wide bar, and a captain's-chair knee raise or cable crunch instead of dead-hang leg raises. The old Elbow Care block is gone — his elbow is doing well, so it's now one short maintenance exercise at the end of each lift day (a different one each day).

✕

What got cut to make room

Day 2's leg press (hip thrust replaces it — legs are covered by squat + RDL + Day 3's leg press and split squat) and Day 3's dumbbell hammer curl (the cable rope version stays).

Session 1

Day 1

Squat + push/pull emphasis. Do the exercises in letter order: A, then B, and so on. Exercises with a number after the letter (B1/B2, D1/D2) are supersets: do one set of B1, short rest, one set of B2, then the longer rest — repeat until all sets are done, then move to C. Tap the sets chip to cross it off as you go (resets when you reload — your real log is in the boxes below each exercise).

Squat

Day 1

Squat · chest · back · shoulders
A · Paused Goblet Squatbiggest calorie-burner of the day + builds squat depth
4 × 8–10
rest 2 min
How: Hold one dumbbell vertically against your chest with both hands. Feet a bit wider than shoulders, toes turned slightly out. Sit down between your legs, chest tall, weight in your heels, down until thighs are at least parallel. Pause 2 seconds at the bottom — gently push your knees out with your elbows, keep the chest up — then drive up.
The pause makes the same weight feel heavier — go about 10–20% lighter than your Phase 1 goblet squat in week 1, then build back up. Heels lifting or knees caving in means go shallower and own that depth first.
B1 · Cable / Pec Deck Chest Flychest — superset with the row
3 × 10–12
rest 30 sec
How: Pec deck: sit tall, forearms/elbows against the pads, bring your arms together in front of your chest in a hugging arc, control back to a light stretch. Cables: handles roughly chest height, one small step forward, keep a soft, fixed bend in the elbows, sweep your arms together like hugging a tree, control back out. Set the elbow bend once and don't change it — the whole rep happens at the shoulder, not the elbow.
Letting the elbows straighten out or drift turns this back into a press — the exact thing the elbow doesn't like. Keep that soft bend locked in the entire time.
B2 · Seated Cable Rowback thickness + posture
3 × 10–12
rest 90 sec
How: Sit tall, slight bend in knees, grab the handle. Pull it to your belly button by driving your elbows back and squeezing your shoulder blades together — like pinching a pencil between them. Let the arms extend fully forward again each rep.
Rowing with your whole body by rocking back and forth. Torso stays mostly still — the back and arms do the work, not momentum.
C · Neutral-Grip Seated Dumbbell Pressdelts — easier on the elbow than the standard grip
3 × 10–12
rest 90 sec
How: Sit with back support. Dumbbells at shoulder height, palms facing each other the whole set (not rotated to face forward) — this hammer grip keeps the forearm and elbow in a more natural line. Press up but stop just short of a full elbow lockout, lower back to ear level under control. Keep your ribs down, don't arch your lower back.
If your lower back arches to push the weight up, it's too heavy.
Still clicks or hurts even with the softer grip and stopping short of lockout? Drop it for now — the Lateral Raises below already cover shoulder width, so skipping the press for a few weeks costs almost nothing.
D1 · Dumbbell Lateral Raise★ your shoulder-width builder — superset with face pulls
4 × 12–15
rest 30 sec
How: Light dumbbells at your sides. With a slight elbow bend, raise them out to the sides until arms are about parallel to the floor — like pouring two small jugs of water. Lead with the elbows, not the hands. Lower slow.
Going too heavy and swinging. Light weight, no momentum — you should feel it burning in the side of your shoulder, not your neck. Don't shrug.
D2 · Cable Face Pull★ posture fix — pull the shoulders back
3 × 15
rest 60 sec
How: Rope attachment set to about face height. Pull the rope toward your forehead, splitting your hands apart as you pull, so your knuckles end up beside your ears and elbows point out. Squeeze the back of your shoulders. This directly counters the rounded-forward sitting posture.
Too heavy turns it into a row. Keep it light and high — the goal is the squeeze at the back, not the load.
E · Plankcore / anti-collapse
3 × 30–45 sec
rest 45 sec
How: Forearms on the floor, body in one straight line from head to heels. Squeeze your glutes and brace your stomach like you're about to be poked. Hold. Breathe normally.
Hips sagging down or piled up in the air. Flat line. When 45 sec is easy, add time before adding anything else.
F · Isometric Wrist Extensor Holdelbow maintenance · calms the forearm tendons that anchor at the elbow
3 × 20–30 sec
rest 30 sec
How: Forearm flat on a bench or table, palm down, hand hanging off the edge. Hold a light dumbbell and keep the wrist bent upward — don't move it, just hold the position. Loads the tendons around the elbow without moving the joint. (Log the seconds in the reps box.)
Should feel like a tolerable burn in the forearm, not pain in the elbow joint. If the elbow itself hurts, drop to no weight — just the hand. Sharp pain or soreness the next morning: skip it and tell Ege.
Cooldowndon't skip it — this is where the mobility gains come from
4 min
Three stretches from the Mobility section: hip flexor, hamstring, child's pose with side reach. Then you're done.

Pull

Day 2

Hinge · back · glutes · incline press
A · Romanian Deadlift (Dumbbells)hamstrings, glutes, whole back chain
3 × 8–10
rest 2–3 min
How: Dumbbells in front of your thighs, feet hip-width. Soft knees (not bent, not locked). Push your hips backward like closing a car door with your butt, letting the dumbbells slide down your legs, back flat the whole time. Go until you feel a stretch in your hamstrings (usually mid-shin), then drive your hips forward to stand tall.
This is a hip movement, NOT a back bend. If your lower back rounds, you've gone too low or too heavy. Keep the weights close to your legs. Hamstring tightness here is a mobility limiter — the cooldown stretch is there for exactly this.
B1 · Neutral-Grip Lat PulldownV-taper — palms facing each other is kinder to the elbow than the wide bar
3 × 8–12
rest 30 sec
How: Use the close/neutral-grip handle (V-bar) or the parallel-grip bars, palms facing each other. Pull it down to your upper chest by driving your elbows down and back, chest up. Think "pull with your elbows, not your hands." Control it all the way back up until your lats stretch.
Weight may feel different from the wide bar — start with a weight you can do clean for 8 and build from there. Leaning way back and yanking turns it into a heave; slight lean only. If your forearm/elbow still grumbles, ease off the grip: think hooks, not fists.
B2 · Neutral-Grip Incline Dumbbell Pressupper chest + front delts — easier on the elbow than the standard grip
3 × 10–12
rest 90 sec
How: Bench set to a low incline (~30°). Dumbbells at upper-chest level with palms facing each other the whole rep (not rotated forward). Press up, stop just short of locking the elbows out, lower under control until you feel a stretch across the chest. Keep your shoulder blades pulled back and down into the bench.
Elbows flaring straight out to 90° stresses the shoulders (and the elbow). Keep them at roughly a 45° angle from your body.
Still clicks even with the neutral grip and shortened range? Drop this one and let the Chest Fly on the other days cover chest — no need to force a press through pain.
C · Hip ThrustNEW · glutes — big muscle, big calorie cost, fixes sitting-all-day hips
3 × 10–12
rest 90 sec
How: Upper back against the edge of a bench, feet flat about hip-width, knees bent. Rest a dumbbell (or barbell with a pad, or use the hip-thrust machine) across your hip crease. Drive through your heels, lift your hips until your torso is flat from shoulders to knees, squeeze the glutes hard for a second, lower under control. Chin tucked, ribs down.
Arching your lower back at the top instead of squeezing your glutes — stop at a flat line. Start light: the movement is easy to learn but easy to cheat, and the weight climbs fast.
D1 · Dumbbell Lateral Raise★ shoulder width — again, every session
4 × 12–15
rest 30 sec
How: Same as Day 1. Light dumbbells, raise out to the sides to shoulder height, lead with the elbows, lower slowly. Side delts grow from doing these often, so they earn a spot all three days.
Neck doing the work. If you feel it in your traps/neck, lighten up and focus on pushing out through the elbows.
D2 · Reverse Pec Deck / Rear-Delt Fly★ posture — the muscles that pull you upright
3 × 15
rest 60 sec
How: On the pec deck machine, sit facing the pad, chest against it, and push the handles out and back in a wide arc — squeezing your rear shoulders and upper back. No machine? Bend at the hips holding light dumbbells and raise them out to the sides.
Using your arms to bend and straighten. Keep a fixed slight elbow bend and move from the shoulder — it's a wide sweeping arc, not a curl.
E · Dead Bugdeep core, safe for the back
3 × 8 / side
rest 45 sec
How: Lie on your back, arms pointing at the ceiling, knees bent at 90° in the air. Slowly lower your opposite arm and leg toward the floor, keeping your lower back pressed flat into the ground, then return. Alternate sides.
Lower back arching up off the floor as you extend. If it lifts, don't reach as far. Flat back is the whole exercise.
F · Slow Wrist Extension (3-sec lower)elbow maintenance · builds tendon tolerance on the outside of the elbow
2 × 12–15
rest 30 sec
How: Forearm on a bench, palm down, hand off the edge, very light dumbbell. Use the other hand to help lift the wrist up, then lower it with just the working arm over a slow 3-count. Full range, no jerking.
Light means light — this should feel almost too easy. Going heavy here is how you irritate the thing you're trying to maintain. If the elbow aches during the set, stop.

Full

Day 3

Legs · back · chest · arms
A · Leg Presslegs — heaviest leg work of the week
4 × 10–12
rest 2 min
How: Feet shoulder-width on the platform, mid-height. Lower the platform by bending your knees until they're about 90°, then press back up — but never lock your knees hard at the top. Keep your lower back flat against the pad the whole time.
Going so deep that your lower back rounds up off the pad — that's where backs get hurt. Stop the descent before your hips tuck under. Don't bounce at the bottom.
B · Dumbbell Split SquatNEW · single-leg strength + stretches the hip flexor under load
3 × 8 / side
rest 90 sec
How: Long stride stance, back heel lifted, dumbbell in each hand at your sides (palms in) — or start with bodyweight and one hand on a rack. Lower straight down until the back knee almost touches the floor, front shin about vertical, torso tall. Push through the front heel to stand. Finish all reps on one leg, then switch.
Front knee caving in, or the stance so short that the front knee shoots way past the toes. Take a longer step. Start with bodyweight in week 1 — balance is part of the job here.
C1 · Seated Cable Rowback + posture — superset with the fly
3 × 10–12
rest 30 sec
How: Same as Day 1. Sit tall, pull to your belly button, drive elbows back, squeeze the shoulder blades, full stretch forward each rep. Torso stays still.
Rounding forward on the stretch. Let the arms extend but keep your chest up and back flat.
C2 · Cable / Pec Deck Chest Flychest, second dose — elbow-safe
3 × 10–12
rest 90 sec
How: Same as Day 1 — set a soft, fixed elbow bend and leave it there the whole set, sweep the arms together in a hugging arc, control the stretch back out.
Don't let the elbows straighten out mid-set, that turns it back into a press.
D1 · Dumbbell Lateral Raise★ shoulder width — third and final dose
4 × 12–15
rest 30 sec
How: You know these now. Light, out to the sides, lead with elbows, slow down. Consistency across all three days is what builds the width.
By Day 3 your shoulders are a little tired — that's fine, drop the weight if needed but keep the reps clean.
D2 · Cable Face Pull★ posture, second dose this week
3 × 15
rest 60 sec
How: Same as Day 1. Rope at face height, pull to your forehead, split the hands, elbows high, squeeze the rear shoulders.
Keep it light and high. If it feels like a heavy row, you've lost the point.
E · Cable Rope Hammer Curlarms — elbow-friendly neutral grip
3 × 12
rest 60 sec
How: Rope attachment low on a cable machine. Hold both ends with a neutral, hammer grip (palms facing each other), elbows pinned to your sides, curl the rope up toward your shoulders, squeeze, lower under control.
Lowering all the way to a hard, locked-out elbow every rep — stop just short of straight. The tension should stay on the muscle, not hang on the joint.
F · Captain's-Chair Knee Raise or Cable Crunchcore finisher — no dead-hang, so the elbow stays happy
3 × 12
rest 45 sec
How: Knee raise: use the captain's-chair pad — forearms on the pads, back against the pad, pull your knees up toward your chest, lower slow, no swinging. Cable crunch: kneel facing a high cable with rope behind your head, crunch down by rounding your spine, not by pulling with your arms.
Swinging on the knee raise. Slow and controlled — if you're using momentum you're training nothing. Skip hanging from a bar for now: a dead hang pulls the elbows straight under your body weight.
G · Forearm Rotations (Pronation / Supination)elbow maintenance · keeps the elbow rotating smoothly under light load
2 × 12 / side
rest 30 sec
How: Elbow tucked at your side (or forearm resting on your thigh), bent to 90°, holding a light dumbbell or hammer by one end. Slowly rotate the forearm palm-up, then palm-down, through the range that's comfortable. Smooth, controlled, no swinging.
Keep it slow and the weight tiny. A painless click through the rotation is fine; a sharp catch means shrink the range. Pain that sticks around: stop and tell Ege.

Mobility cooldown + off-day flow

Mobility only sticks with repetition, not intensity. Stretch to a pull of about 6/10 — never pain, never bouncing — and breathe out slowly as you settle in. If a position gets easy, hold it longer rather than forcing it deeper. Priorities for a desk-sitter: hips, mid-back, ankles, hamstrings.
